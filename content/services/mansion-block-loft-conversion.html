Loft conversions

Mansion Block & Flat Loft Conversion in London

A loft conversion for a flat or mansion block in London can turn a top-floor apartment into a duplex with an extra bedroom, bathroom or living space upstairs. Flats need more permissions than houses, so we help you through planning, freeholder consent and the build as one joined-up process.

What's included

What a Flat Loft Conversion Includes

  • Feasibility survey of the roof, access and the space above your flat.
  • Lease review guidance: what to ask your solicitor about the demise and the freeholder's consent.
  • Planning application, including heritage statements for listed buildings or conservation areas.

  • Building Regulations sign-off, including fire separation between flats.

Is it right for you?

Signs a Loft Conversion Could Work for Your Flat

  • You own the top-floor flat, with the roof space directly above you.
  • Other flats on your street or in your block have already converted their lofts.
  • You share the freehold, or your freeholder is open to selling or leasing the roof space.
  • You would rather add space than sell, given the cost of moving in London.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost of a Flat Loft Conversion?

  • The freeholder's premium for the loft space, which can be the largest single cost.
  • Legal and surveyor fees, including the freeholder's costs, which leases usually require you to pay.
  • The conversion type: often a mansard or rooflight design to satisfy planning.
  • Access: scaffolding over shared entrances and working around other residents.
  • Heritage requirements: natural slate, timber windows and matching details in conservation areas.

Local knowledge

Mansion Block Loft Conversions in London

London's red-brick mansion blocks, built mostly between the 1880s and 1910s, are concentrated in areas such as Maida Vale, Marylebone, Kensington, Battersea and West Hampstead. Many are listed or within conservation areas, and their roofs are shared by every flat in the block. The Planning Portal confirms that flats and maisonettes need planning permission for a loft conversion.1 Camden Council's leaseholder guidance gives an example of a freeholder premium of £70,000 on a £200,000 increase in value.2
