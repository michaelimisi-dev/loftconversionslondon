Is it right for you?

Signs a Terrace or Balcony Suits Your Loft

  • Your loft faces a garden, park or skyline view.
  • You have a flat-roofed back addition below the new loft.
  • Your house has little or no garden and you want outdoor space.
  • Neighbouring houses already have loft balconies or terraces, which helps with planning.
  • You want the light of a full-height door without the planning risk: a Juliet balcony is the answer.

Local knowledge

Loft Terraces and Overlooking in London

London's tightly packed terraces mean back gardens are close together, so overlooking is the main reason councils refuse loft terraces. Government guidance treats any raised platform over 0.3 metres high, including roof terraces, as needing planning permission, while a Juliet balcony with no platform would normally be permitted development.1 Designs that set the terrace back from the edge or add obscure screens often succeed where a full-width deck would not.

Guarding Built to Regulations

Approved Document K requires guarding of at least 1,100 mm on balconies and roof terraces, designed so children cannot easily climb it. Every balustrade we fit meets that height, using toughened laminated glass or steel, and is fixed into the structure rather than just the decking.
