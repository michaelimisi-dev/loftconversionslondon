Loft conversions

Bungalow Loft Conversion in London

A bungalow loft conversion in London can almost double your living space without building on the garden. Bungalows have a large roof over the whole footprint, so the loft can hold two bedrooms and a bathroom, leaving the ground floor free for living space.

  • Turns a bungalow into a chalet-style home
  • Often permitted development up to 50 m³
  • Dormer, hip-to-gable or rooflight designs
  • Staircase designed around your layout

What's included

What a Bungalow Loft Conversion Includes

  • Survey and design of the roof, the ground-floor layout and the staircase position.
  • Structural engineering for the new floor, any steels and the roof alterations.
  • Approvals: lawful development certificate or planning, and Building Regulations.
  • Roof work: dormers, hip-to-gable or rooflights, depending on your roof shape.
  • A new staircase and the alterations needed downstairs to fit it.
  • Fit-out: insulation, heating, electrics, a bathroom if wanted, and plastering.
  • Fire safety and sign-off with building control.

Is it right for you?

Signs Your Bungalow Is Ready for a Loft Conversion

  • The roof is reasonably steep, giving height in the middle of the loft.
  • You want more bedrooms but do not want to lose garden to an extension.
  • Neighbouring bungalows already have dormers or rooflights.
  • You can give up part of a room or hallway for a staircase.
  • You would like to keep living on one level, with guests or family upstairs.

Our process

How We Convert a Bungalow Loft

  1. Free survey and staircase options.
  2. Design and approvals.
  3. Scaffold and roof work: dormers or gables built from outside.
  4. New floor structure spanning the bungalow's walls.

Pricing

How Much Does a Bungalow Loft Conversion Cost?

Checkatrade puts a typical 50 m² bungalow loft conversion at around £75,000, within a range of about £62,500 to £87,500.1 These are UK figures, so London projects usually sit towards the top. The cost depends on:

  • Floor area: bungalow lofts are larger than most, so the total is higher even if the rate per square metre is similar.
  • Roof design: rooflights cost least and a raised roof costs most.
  • Staircase: including any changes to walls and rooms on the ground floor.
  • Bathroom: usually placed above the existing bathroom to keep drainage simple.

Local knowledge

Bungalow Loft Conversions in Outer London

Bungalows are rare in inner London but common in outer boroughs such as Bromley, Bexley, Havering, Hillingdon and Harrow, many built between the 1930s and 1960s on generous plots. Their large hipped roofs make them well suited to a hip-to-gable or rear dormer conversion. A detached or semi-detached bungalow has a permitted development allowance of 50 cubic metres, which is often enough for two bedrooms and a bathroom.2
