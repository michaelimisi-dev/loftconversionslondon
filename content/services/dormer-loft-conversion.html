Loft conversions

Dormer Loft Conversion in London

If you need a dormer loft conversion in London, this is the most popular way to turn an unused roof space into a full-height bedroom, en-suite or office. A rear dormer extends vertically out of the back roof slope, so the front of your house looks the same from the street.

  • Usually permitted development, with no planning application
  • Full standing height across the rear of the loft
  • Suits terraced, semi-detached and detached houses
  • Typically 6 to 8 weeks on site

What's included

What a London Dormer Loft Conversion Includes

A dormer loft conversion is more than a box on the roof. A complete conversion covers every stage needed to turn the roof space into a legal habitable room that you can sell with confidence later:

  • Survey and design: measured survey, layout options and planning drawings.
  • Structural engineering: calculations for the new floor joists and steel beams, which carry the dormer's weight onto the walls rather than the ceilings below.
  • Approvals: a lawful development certificate or planning application, Building Regulations approval and party wall notices where needed.
  • The dormer structure: timber frame, insulated flat roof, external finish such as tile hanging or render to match your house, and windows.
  • Inside the loft: new floor, insulation, plasterboard, electrics, heating, and a staircase that meets Building Regulations.
  • Fire safety: a protected escape route, fire doors and linked smoke alarms on every floor.
  • Sign-off: building control's final inspection and your completion certificate.

Is it right for you?

Signs a Dormer Is the Right Loft Conversion for Your Home

A rear dormer suits more London houses than any other loft type. It is likely to be your best option if:

  • Your loft has a reasonable ridge height but the sloping roof leaves too little standing room.
  • You want the most space you can get without changing the front of the house.
  • You want a bathroom in the loft. The flat ceiling of the dormer gives the headroom a shower needs.
  • Your house is not in a conservation area, or your council accepts rear dormers on your street.
  • You need the extra bedroom within months, not a year. Permitted development avoids the 8-week planning wait.

If your house has a hipped roof that slopes at the side, a dormer alone may leave the loft too narrow. In that case a hip-to-gable loft conversion combined with a rear dormer usually works better.

Our process

How We Build a Dormer Loft Conversion

  1. Free survey. We measure the loft, check the ridge height, look at the roof timbers and agree where the staircase should land.
  2. Drawings and calculations. An architectural designer draws the dormer and a structural engineer specifies the beams and joists.
  3. Approvals. We confirm the dormer fits permitted development and apply for a lawful development certificate, which proves it to future buyers. Party wall notices go to your neighbours at least two months before work on a shared wall.
  4. Scaffold and roof. Scaffolding goes up, the rear roof slope is opened and the dormer frame, roof and windows are built from outside.
  5. Floor and steels. New floor joists and steel beams go in at loft level, independent of the ceilings below.
  6. Fit-out. Insulation, electrics, plumbing, plasterboard, and finally the staircase cut through into the landing.
  7. Sign-off. Building control inspects and issues your completion certificate.

Pricing

How Much Does a Dormer Loft Conversion Cost in London?

Independent London price guides put a rear dormer at roughly £40,000 to £90,000. MyJobQuote gives about £40,500 to £57,500 for a 30 m² London dormer, while PlanWatch puts a typical London rear dormer at £60,000 to £90,000.1, 2 The main things that move the price are:

  • Size: a full-width dormer costs more than a partial one, but usually less per square metre.
  • Roof structure: older cut-timber roofs are simpler to convert than modern trussed roofs, which need more steelwork.
  • Staircase position: fitting the new stair over the existing one is cheapest. Moving walls on the landing adds cost.
  • An en-suite: plumbing and drainage depend on how close the bathroom sits to your existing soil pipe.
  • Windows and finish: extra rooflights, Juliet doors and high-spec joinery all add up.

Local knowledge

Dormer Loft Conversions on London Houses

London's Victorian and Edwardian terraces were built with steep slate roofs and solid party walls, which is why rear dormers are such a familiar sight across the city. Permitted development has conditions, though. The dormer must be set back at least 20 cm from the original eaves where practicable, side-facing windows must be obscure-glazed and fixed shut below 1.7 metres, and materials must look similar to the house.3 Large parts of inner London are conservation areas, where roof extensions are not permitted development, so checking your street is the first thing we do.

Permitted Development Checked First

Before you spend anything on drawings, we calculate the dormer's volume against the 40 cubic metre limit for terraced houses or 50 cubic metres for other houses, including any earlier roof work. We also check for conservation areas and Article 4 directions. If your dormer fits, we apply for a lawful development certificate so a future buyer's solicitor has proof.

2 m

Staircases That Pass Building Control

The staircase decides whether a loft feels like part of the house. We plan it at the survey so it meets Approved Document K, which requires 2 metres of headroom over the stair, or 1.9 metres at the centre and 1.8 metres at the side under a sloping roof. Getting this right early is what stops costly redesigns halfway through the build.

0.15

A Dormer That Stays Warm

Flat-roofed dormers can be cold if they are insulated poorly. Every dormer we design meets the Building Regulations limit of 0.15 W/m²K for a new roof, which is the maximum rate of heat loss allowed. That keeps the new room comfortable in winter and stops heat escaping from the bedrooms below.
