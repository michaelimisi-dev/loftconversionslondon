Loft Pod Conversion in London

A loft pod conversion in London adds a compact room over the back addition of a terraced house. It is the L section of an L-shaped dormer on its own: ideal for an en-suite next to an existing loft bedroom, a quiet study, or a small extra bedroom.

  • Adds a room over your back addition
  • Can extend an existing loft conversion
  • Perfect for an en-suite or study
  • Party wall and permitted development checks included

What's included

What a Loft Pod Conversion Includes

  • Survey of the outrigger roof, walls and how the pod will connect to the main loft or landing.
  • Volume check against permitted development, counting any loft work already done.
  • Structural design for the pod floor, frame and any steel that bears on the party wall.
  • Approvals: lawful development certificate or planning, Building Regulations and party wall notices.
  • The pod itself: frame, insulated flat roof, external finish and window.
  • Fit-out: insulation, electrics, plastering, and plumbing if it is an en-suite.
  • Sign-off by building control.

Is it right for you?

Signs a Loft Pod Is the Right Choice

  • You already have a loft bedroom but no bathroom on that floor.
  • You need a small extra room, not a whole new storey.
  • Your terrace has a back addition with its own lower pitched roof.
  • You want to add space in stages, starting with a pod and adding a main dormer later, or the other way round.

If you are starting from an unconverted loft and want the most space, a full L-shaped dormer built in one go is usually better value.

Our process

How We Build a Loft Pod

  1. Free survey of the back addition and the connection point.
  2. Design, volume check and approvals, including party wall notices.
  3. Scaffold over the back addition.
  4. Roof opened and pod built, usually within a few weeks.
  5. Connection to the existing loft or landing, finished to match.
  6. Fit-out and building control sign-off.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost of a Loft Pod in London?

Because pods vary so much in size and use, we price each one after the survey. The main cost factors are:

  • Size and how much of the outrigger roof it covers.
  • Use: an en-suite costs more than a study because of plumbing, tiling and extraction.
  • Connection: joining onto an existing dormer is different from creating a new opening from the landing.
  • Party wall fees if your neighbour appoints their own surveyor.
  • Doing it alongside a main dormer: building both together shares scaffold, design and approval costs.

Local knowledge

Loft Pods on London Terraces

Many London families converted the main loft years ago with a simple rear dormer and now want a second bathroom. The back addition behind most Victorian and Edwardian terraces is the natural place for it, as it usually sits above the existing kitchen or bathroom. Under permitted development, roof work that joins the roof of a rear extension is exempt from the usual 20 cm eaves set-back, which is what lets a pod make full use of the outrigger.1

Why Choose Us for Your Loft Pod

Earlier Loft Work Counted

Permitted development's 40 cubic metre limit for terraced houses includes any roof extension built before. If you already have a dormer, we calculate exactly how much volume remains before designing the pod, so you know at the start whether it needs planning permission.

15 l/s

En-Suites That Do Not Steam Up

Building Regulations Approved Document F requires an intermittent extract rate of at least 15 litres per second in a bathroom. We design pod en-suites with proper extraction, waterproof tanking behind the tiles and a drainage route that falls correctly to the existing stack.

A pod has to look and feel like part of the loft, not a bolt-on. At one survey visit we check how the existing conversion was built, so the new floor levels, ceiling heights and finishes match the room it joins.
