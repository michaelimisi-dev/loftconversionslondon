Loft conversions

Mansard Loft Conversion in London

A mansard loft conversion in London gives you more space than any other type of loft. The rear roof slope is rebuilt as a near-vertical wall at about 72 degrees, with windows set into it, creating a full extra storey that looks at home on a London terrace.

  • The most floor space of any loft conversion
  • A familiar, accepted look on London streets
  • Planning application prepared and submitted for you
  • Typically 12 to 16 weeks on site

What's included

What a London Mansard Conversion Includes

  • Survey and planning drawings that follow your council's guidance on mansard roofs.
  • A full planning application, including the design statement and any heritage information your council asks for.
  • Structural design for the new roof, floor joists, steel beams and raised party walls.
  • Party wall notices to neighbours on both sides where walls are raised or cut into.
  • The mansard structure: steep rear slope finished in slate or tiles, a flat top roof, and windows in small dormers or set flush.
  • Fit-out: floor, insulation, services, plastering, staircase, fire doors and smoke alarms.
  • Sign-off by building control with a completion certificate.

Is it right for you?

Signs a Mansard Is the Right Loft for Your Home

  • You want two good bedrooms or a large suite in the loft, not one compact room.
  • Your house is on a terraced street where neighbours already have mansards.
  • Your loft is too low for a dormer to give comfortable headroom.
  • You live in a conservation area where your council prefers mansards over box dormers.
  • You are happy to wait for planning permission to get the biggest possible room.

Our process

How We Build a Mansard Loft Conversion

  1. Free survey and a check of your council's mansard guidance and any nearby approvals.
  2. Planning drawings and application. Councils aim to decide householder applications within 8 weeks.
  3. Structural design and Building Regulations approval once planning is granted.
  4. Party wall notices served at least two months before work on the shared walls.
  5. Scaffold and temporary roof so the house stays watertight while the old roof comes off.
  6. New structure: party walls raised, floor and steels installed, mansard frame and roof built.
  7. Fit-out and sign-off, ending with building control's completion certificate.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost of a Mansard in London?

  • Front and rear, or rear only: a double mansard rebuilds both slopes and costs considerably more.
  • Party walls: raising walls on both sides adds brickwork and surveyor fees.
  • Roof finish: natural slate, which many councils require, costs more than concrete tiles.
  • Windows: timber sash windows in conservation areas cost more than standard casements.
  • Access: scaffolding and skips on narrow inner London streets may need council licences.

Local knowledge

Mansard Roofs and London Planning

Mansard roofs have been built in London since the 17th century and are part of the character of Georgian and Victorian terraces in boroughs such as Islington, Camden, Kensington and Chelsea, and Westminster. Because so many streets already have them, several inner London councils publish design guidance describing the mansard they will accept, typically with a 70 to 72 degree lower slope, a flat top hidden behind the parapet, and windows set in small dormers. Following that guidance from the outset gives your application the best chance of approval.

Planning Applications Done Properly

Councils aim to decide householder planning applications within 8 weeks, and a poorly drawn application can be refused and start that clock again. We design your mansard to your council's own guidance, reference approved mansards on your street, and include everything the planning officer needs, so the first application is the only one.

2 sides

Party Walls on Both Sides Managed

A mid-terrace mansard usually raises the party walls on both sides, which means two neighbours to notify under the Party Wall etc. Act 1996, each with at least two months' notice. We help you plan the notices around the planning decision, so the build starts on time.

12 to 16 wks

The House Kept Watertight

A mansard takes 12 to 16 weeks on site and the old roof comes off completely. A temporary roof over the scaffold keeps the house dry throughout, so you can keep living at home while the new storey is built.
