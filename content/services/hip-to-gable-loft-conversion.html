Loft conversions

Hip-to-Gable Loft Conversion in London

A hip-to-gable loft conversion in London is the answer for houses whose roof slopes down at the side as well as the front and back. We build the side wall straight up to the ridge, turning a narrow triangular loft into a room wide enough for a proper staircase and a double bedroom.

  • Ideal for 1930s semis and end-of-terrace houses
  • Often permitted development
  • Usually combined with a rear dormer for full height
  • Typically 6 to 8 weeks on site

What's included

What a Hip-to-Gable Conversion Includes

A hip-to-gable conversion changes the shape of your roof, so the structure and the weatherproofing matter as much as the room inside. A complete project covers:

  • Survey and design: measured survey, roof structure check, layout options and drawings.
  • Structural engineering: calculations for the new gable wall, ridge beam, floor joists and any steels bearing on the party wall.
  • Approvals: lawful development certificate or planning application, Building Regulations approval and party wall notices.
  • The gable wall: built up from the existing side wall in brick or blockwork and rendered or faced to match, with the hipped roof section rebuilt to the ridge.
  • Optional rear dormer: the usual partner to a hip-to-gable, adding full standing height across the back.
  • Fit-out: floor, insulation, electrics, heating, plastering, a staircase and fire-safe escape route.
  • Sign-off: building control inspections and a completion certificate.

Is it right for you?

Signs Your London Home Needs a Hip-to-Gable Conversion

  • Your roof slopes down on the side of the house, not just the front and back. This is called a hipped roof.
  • Standing in the loft, the space narrows to a point at one or both ends.
  • There is nowhere for a staircase to arrive with enough headroom.
  • You live in a semi-detached, end-of-terrace or detached house. Mid-terraces have no side roof to convert.
  • A rear dormer alone would leave you with a small, awkward room.

Our process

How a Hip-to-Gable Conversion Is Built

  1. Free survey of the roof, the side wall and the space below, so we know the wall can carry the new gable.
  2. Design and calculations for the gable, beams and floor.
  3. Approvals, including a lawful development certificate and party wall notices to your attached neighbour.
  4. Scaffold on the side and rear of the house.
  5. Gable build: the hipped section is stripped, the gable wall is built up to the ridge and the roof is extended to meet it.
  6. Dormer and floor: the rear dormer goes in, followed by the new floor joists and steels.
  7. Fit-out and sign-off: insulation, services, staircase, fire doors and building control's final inspection.

Pricing

What Affects the Cost of a Hip-to-Gable in London?

  • The side wall: the gable is built on your existing wall, so its condition and foundations matter.
  • Roof covering: matching clay or concrete tiles on 1930s houses is usually straightforward. Rare or reclaimed tiles cost more.
  • Adding a dormer: typically £8,000 to £15,000 more, but it often doubles the usable floor area.2
  • Party wall costs: if your neighbour appoints their own surveyor, you pay their fee.
  • Bathroom and finish: an en-suite and higher-spec joinery add to the total.

Local knowledge

Hip-to-Gable Conversions on London's 1930s Semis

Outer London boroughs were filled with semi-detached houses in the interwar building boom, and many of them have hipped roofs. That is why hip-to-gable conversions are so common in places like Ealing, Barnet, Bexley and Hounslow. The Planning Portal confirms that hip-to-gable work is one of the exceptions to the 20 cm eaves set-back rule, and that it counts towards the 40 or 50 cubic metre volume limit.3 Some councils protect matching pairs of semis in conservation areas, so we always check your street first.

Why choose us

Why Choose Us for Your Hip-to-Gable Conversion

50 m³

Volume Calculated Before You Commit

A hip-to-gable plus a rear dormer can come close to the 50 cubic metre permitted development limit for a semi-detached house. We calculate the volume from the survey, including any earlier roof work, before drawings start. If it is too close, we adjust the dormer so you avoid a planning application or a refusal.

2 months

Neighbours Notified Properly

On a semi-detached house, the steels for a hip-to-gable usually bear onto the party wall. The Party Wall etc. Act 1996 requires at least two months' notice for that work. We help you serve notices early, so the build is not delayed while you wait for your neighbour's response.

6 to 8 wks

A Realistic Build Programme

Published guides put a hip-to-gable at 6 to 8 weeks on site. We give you a written programme that shows when the scaffold goes up, when the roof is open and when the staircase goes in, so you know exactly when the house will be disrupted.
