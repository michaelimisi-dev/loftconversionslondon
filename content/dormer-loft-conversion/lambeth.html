Local knowledge

What Makes Dormer Conversions in Lambeth Different

Lambeth stretches from the Georgian and early Victorian streets of Kennington and Stockwell, through the Victorian terraces of Brixton and Herne Hill, to the interwar suburbs of Streatham and West Norwood. Each has a different roof, and Lambeth's Design Guide treats them differently.1

The guide is unusually specific about dormers. They should be subordinate to the roof, lower than the windows on the elevation below and set in from the sides of the roof. They should be of modest size and aligned with the windows underneath, with a front face that contains only window and no wall. Bulky timber fascias and bargeboards should be avoided.1 Interestingly, the guide calls dormers the most appropriate way to add roof space in conservation areas, where larger roof extensions are usually resisted.

Planning in Lambeth

Planning a Dormer in Lambeth

  • Rear, not front: Lambeth generally supports rear dormers and resists front ones where they are not characteristic of the street.1
  • Outside conservation areas: a rear dormer may be permitted development within 40 cubic metres on a terrace or 50 on other houses.2
  • Conservation areas: a planning application is needed. Lambeth publishes Article 4 information in its conservation area profiles and under the "constraints" tab of its planning search.3
  • Protected estates: some, such as the Leigham Court Estate in Streatham, have Article 4 directions controlling changes to their character.4
