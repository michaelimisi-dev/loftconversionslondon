Local knowledge

What Makes Dormer Conversions in Lewisham Different

Lewisham's biggest single source of loft conversions is the Corbett Estate, a neighbourhood of around 3,000 houses between Hither Green and Catford. Archibald Corbett bought North Park Farm in 1895, the same year Hither Green station opened, and built street after street of terraced houses for City commuters.1 Their consistent roofs and rear returns make them ideal for rear dormers, and many streets already have them.

Further north, the picture changes. Brockley, Telegraph Hill, Ladywell and Hatcham are conservation areas with Article 4 directions, and Lewisham's list of controlled works in these areas includes alterations to the roof of a house.2 Brockley's direction, made in 2006, aims to keep the area's original architectural features.3 Dormers are still possible there, but they need a planning application and careful design.

Planning in Lewisham

Lewisham's Dormer Guidance

Lewisham's Alterations and Extensions SPD, adopted in 2019, illustrates what it will and will not accept:4

  • Acceptable: slender, subordinate dormers aligned with the windows below.
  • Unacceptable: bulky dormers with a poor relationship to the elevation below.
  • Two dormers: acceptable if they read as separate elements and the connecting section does not dominate.
  • Large full-width dormers: only acceptable outside conservation areas, and set back from the eaves.

Outside conservation areas, a rear dormer within 40 cubic metres on a terrace or 50 on other houses is often permitted development.5
