Local knowledge

What Makes Dormer Conversions in Haringey Different

The Harringay Ladder, the grid of parallel streets between Green Lanes and Wightman Road, was laid out by the British Land Company in 1880 to 1881, and the area was fully built up by 1900. Several builders worked there, which is why the Ladder's houses vary more in style than a typical estate.1 For a dormer, that means no two surveys are quite the same: roof pitch, rear return and party wall details change from street to street.

Elsewhere, Haringey's 28 conservation areas cover much of Crouch End, Muswell Hill, Highgate and the planned estates of Noel Park and Tower Gardens.2 In these areas the consistent form of the roofs is part of what is protected, so a rear dormer needs a planning application and a design that respects the terrace. Outside them, Haringey routinely confirms rear dormers as permitted development: one example is a lawful development certificate for a loft conversion with rear dormers on Clyde Road, Wood Green, in early 2025.3

Planning in Haringey

Planning a Dormer in Haringey

  • Outside conservation areas: often permitted development within 40 cubic metres on a terrace, set back from the eaves and finished in similar materials.4
  • Conservation areas: a planning application is needed. Rear dormers can be accepted, but front roof extensions are generally considered unsuitable.
  • Lawful development certificate: we apply for one even when no planning is needed, so a future buyer's solicitor has proof.
