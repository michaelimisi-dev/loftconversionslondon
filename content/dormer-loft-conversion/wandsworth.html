Local knowledge

What Makes Dormer Conversions in Wandsworth Different

Wandsworth was largely built up between the 1870s and 1914, as the railways reached Clapham Junction, Balham and Earlsfield. The result is a borough dominated by two-storey terraced houses with slate roofs and back additions, the classic candidate for a rear dormer. On streets between Wandsworth and Clapham Commons and across Tooting and Earlsfield, rear dormers are now so common that a new one usually fits in with the roofscape behind.

The catch is conservation. Wandsworth has dozens of conservation areas, and in eight of them Article 4 directions remove further permitted development rights.1 On the Latchmere Estate, for example, the direction means changes to roofs, including re-roofing in a different material, need planning permission.2 On the Shaftesbury Park Estate, the 1870s "workmen's city" of small two-storey cottages built by the Artizans', Labourers' and General Dwellings Company, a dormer has to respect a very uniform roofline.3

Planning in Wandsworth

Planning a Dormer in Wandsworth

  • Outside conservation areas: a rear dormer is often permitted development within 40 cubic metres on a terrace. We confirm it with a lawful development certificate.4
  • In a conservation area: a planning application is needed, but approvals do happen. Wandsworth approved a main rear roof dormer with two small front dormers in the Wandsworth Town Conservation Area in January 2025.5
  • On an Article 4 estate: expect closer scrutiny of materials and roof shape, and design to the estate's appraisal from the start.
  • Council guidance: Wandsworth's Housing SPD includes a section on roof alterations and loft conversions, which we design to.6
