Local knowledge

What Makes Dormer Conversions in Barnet Different

Much of Barnet was farmland until the Northern line arrived. Golders Green station opened in 1907, and Edgware followed in 1924, after which Edgware, Burnt Oak, Mill Hill and Hendon were covered with semi-detached and detached houses through the 1920s and 1930s.1 Those houses mostly have hipped roofs, so a Barnet dormer often goes hand in hand with a hip-to-gable.

The planning route matters more in Barnet than in many boroughs. Barnet's Residential Design Guidance SPD, adopted in 2016 and still applied at appeal, expects dormers to be subordinate features on the roof. Planning inspectors have quoted it as seeking dormers that do not wrap around a hipped roof or take up more than half the width of the roof.2 A permitted development dormer can be considerably larger, so we always check first whether your house still has its permitted development rights.

Planning in Barnet

Planning a Dormer in Barnet

  • Permitted development: within 40 cubic metres on a terrace or 50 on other houses, below the ridge and set back from the eaves.3
  • Planning application: designed to Barnet's 2016 SPD, with a subordinate dormer.
  • Article 4 areas: Barnet has 10 conservation areas covered by Article 4 directions.4
  • Hampstead Garden Suburb: roof alterations need planning permission and the Suburb Trust's consent.5
