Local knowledge

What Makes Dormer Conversions in Waltham Forest Different

Walthamstow owes much of its housing to one family. Thomas Courtenay Warner inherited his father's estate in 1875 and his companies went on to build thousands of homes. Warner houses from 1870 to 1914 are two-storey terraces in yellow brick with red detailing and slate roofs, and many were built as two flats, one upstairs and one down.1 That detail matters for a loft: permitted development rights do not apply to flats, so converting the loft of an upper Warner flat needs a planning application.

For whole houses, a rear dormer is often permitted development. Where permission is needed, Waltham Forest's draft Residential Alterations and Retrofit guidance is precise: dormers should be at least 0.25 metres from the ridge and eaves and 0.5 metres from the sides of the roof, materials should be the highest quality available, and front dormers are typically not supported.2

Planning in Waltham Forest

Planning a Dormer in Waltham Forest

  • Permitted development first: within 40 cubic metres on a terrace or 50 on other houses.3
  • Set-backs if planning is needed: 0.25 m from ridge and eaves, 0.5 m from the roof sides.2
  • Already extended to the side: the dormer should not span the full, wider roof. It is limited to the original roof width, or split into two smaller dormers.2
  • Conservation areas: rear dormers may be supported if they are of exemplar design. Walthamstow Village and Leytonstone are among the areas with Article 4 directions.4
