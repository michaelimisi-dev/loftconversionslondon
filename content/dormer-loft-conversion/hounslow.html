Local knowledge

What Makes Dormer Conversions in Hounslow Different

Hounslow is two boroughs in one. In the east, Chiswick and Brentford have Victorian terraces such as the Glebe Estate, Chiswick's first working-class housing estate, and the famous Bedford Park garden suburb.1 In the west, Feltham and Hounslow West are mostly interwar and early post-war two-storey terraces and semi-detached houses.2 A rear dormer suits both, but the design differs.

Where planning permission is needed, Hounslow's residential extension guidelines are precise. On a terraced or small semi-detached house, a rear dormer should sit at least 0.3 metres below the ridge, 0.5 metres above the eaves and 0.5 metres from the sides of the roof; on larger houses, at least one metre. It should sit centrally on the roof face, and a dormer made wider to fit a staircase will normally be refused.3

Planning in Hounslow

Planning a Dormer in Hounslow

  • Permitted development: within 40 cubic metres on a terrace or 50 on other houses, outside conservation areas.4
  • Planning application: set-ins of 0.3 m, 0.5 m and 0.5 m on smaller houses, one metre on larger ones, and no full-width boxes.3
  • Front dormers: generally refused because of their effect on the street.
  • Bedford Park and Gunnersbury Park: Article 4 directions confirmed in 2001 cover every property.5
