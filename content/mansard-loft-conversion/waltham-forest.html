Local knowledge

Why Mansards Are Rare in Waltham Forest

Unlike inner London, where mansards have topped terraces since the Georgian period, Waltham Forest's housing is mostly late Victorian and Edwardian two-storey terraces with simple pitched slate roofs, plus interwar semis further north. The Warner homes that define much of Walthamstow are a good example: two-storey terraces in yellow brick with slate roofs.1 A mansard on one of these streets would look very different from its neighbours.

Waltham Forest's draft Residential Alterations and Retrofit guidance says so directly: the mansard is not a typical or characteristic roof profile in the borough, so it is unlikely to be appropriate, and may be supported only where it reflects local character and is of exemplar design.2 In practice, that means a mansard is only worth pursuing where similar roofs already exist nearby.

Planning in Waltham Forest

Better Options in Waltham Forest

  • Rear dormer: often permitted development, and the most common loft in the borough.
  • L-shaped dormer: may be supported where it reflects local character, and gives two rooms on a terrace with a back addition.2
  • Raising the ridge: the guidance says this may be supported if it does not harm the streetscape.2
