Local knowledge

Mansards on Lewisham's Older Terraces

The northern edge of Lewisham, around Deptford, New Cross and St John's, has some of the borough's oldest houses: early and mid-Victorian terraces, many with "London roofs" that slope inwards to a hidden valley behind a front parapet. Lewisham's SPD lists London roofs among the borough's common roof types.1 Because these roofs are low and invisible from the street, a dormer is often impractical, and a mansard is the usual way to add a storey.

Lewisham's guidance describes the mansard it wants: a double-pitched roof with four slopes, the lower faces steeply pitched and the upper slopes much shallower. Within conservation areas, it expects this traditional style where a mansard is appropriate and generally resists modern variations, unless they are already the recurring pattern on the street.1

Further south, in Catford, Hither Green and Lee, most terraces have ordinary pitched roofs and rear returns. On those houses a rear dormer or L-shaped dormer usually gives similar space for less cost and no planning application, so we only recommend a mansard where it genuinely suits the house and the street.

Planning in Lewisham

Planning a Mansard in Lewisham

  • Full planning permission is needed for almost every mansard.
  • Street pattern counts: where neighbouring houses already have mansards, Lewisham expects a new one to follow their form.
  • Article 4 areas: Telegraph Hill, Hatcham, Brockley, St Mary's and others have directions that control roof alterations.2
  • Overly bulky designs that dominate the original house are shown as unacceptable in the SPD.
