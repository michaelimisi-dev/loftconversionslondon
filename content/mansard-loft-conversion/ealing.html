Local knowledge

Where Mansards Fit in Ealing

Ealing has fewer of the tall inner-London terraces where mansards are traditional. Its Victorian and Edwardian housing in Acton, central Ealing and West Ealing is mostly two-storey terraces and semi-detached villas with pitched roofs, and the interwar suburbs further west are almost entirely hipped-roof semis. On most of these houses, a mansard would look out of place.

Ealing's residential extensions guidance reflects that. It says roof extensions should be carefully designed because of their prominence in the roofscape, and that extensions which change the slope of the roof will not usually be acceptable.1 In practice, that means a mansard in Ealing is most likely to succeed on a terrace where neighbouring houses already have one, so the new roof continues an established pattern rather than breaking it.

Planning in Ealing

Planning a Mansard in Ealing

  • Full planning permission is needed for almost every mansard.
  • Changing the roof slope is not usually acceptable under Ealing's guidance, so street precedent is crucial.1
  • Conservation areas: Ealing expects roof extensions there to be particularly sensitively designed, and some, such as Brentham, have Article 4 directions.2
  • Alternatives: a rear dormer set within the slope, or rooflights, are far more likely to be approved.
