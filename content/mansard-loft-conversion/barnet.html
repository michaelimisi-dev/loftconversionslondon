Local knowledge

Where a Mansard Makes Sense in Barnet

Barnet's older terraces are concentrated in a few places: around East Finchley and the Great North Road, in New Barnet and the old town of Chipping Barnet, and along the Cricklewood and Childs Hill edge of the borough. These Victorian and Edwardian streets have pitched slate roofs and rear returns, and on the taller houses a rear mansard can add more usable space than a dormer.

Elsewhere the borough is dominated by interwar semi-detached housing, and in Hampstead Garden Suburb, designated a conservation area in 1968, an Article 4 direction removes permitted development rights for any alteration to the roof.1 Barnet's 2016 Residential Design Guidance expects roof extensions to be subordinate to the original roof, a test a full mansard can struggle with unless similar roofs already exist on the street.2 Where a terrace sits on a slope, as many do around East Finchley, a mansard has to step down with the street, so we take levels from the neighbouring roofs at the survey.

Planning in Barnet

Planning a Mansard in Barnet

  • Full planning permission is needed for almost every mansard.
  • Subordinate design: Barnet's guidance expects roof extensions not to dominate the house.2
  • Street precedent: existing mansards on neighbouring houses are the strongest support for an application.
  • Article 4 areas: Barnet has 10 conservation areas covered by Article 4 directions.3
