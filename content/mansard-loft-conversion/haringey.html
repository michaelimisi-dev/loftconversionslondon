Local knowledge

Mansards and Haringey's Planned Estates

Haringey has two of London's most important planned housing estates, and both shape what is possible with a mansard. Noel Park in Wood Green was built by the Artizans, Labourers and General Dwellings Company between about 1881 and 1913, with around 2,000 terraced houses for skilled workers.1 Tower Gardens in Tottenham was one of the London County Council's first cottage estates, built in the Arts and Crafts style from 1904, and is a conservation area with an Article 4 direction.2 On both, the uniform rooflines are the point, so mansards are rarely appropriate.

Away from these estates, the large Victorian terraces of Harringay, Stroud Green and West Green have taller roofs and deeper plans, and are better candidates. A mansard there can add a full storey with two bedrooms, especially where neighbours have already set a precedent on the street. Some Stroud Green and Harringay terraces are three storeys already, and adding a mansard on top makes a four-storey house, which brings stricter fire escape requirements we design in from the start.

Planning in Haringey

Planning a Mansard in Haringey

  • Full planning permission is needed for almost every mansard.
  • Conservation areas: Haringey has 28, and in them the consistency of the roofs is a key part of what is protected.3
  • Street precedent: approved mansards on neighbouring houses strengthen an application, and we match their form.
