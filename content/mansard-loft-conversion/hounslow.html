Local knowledge

Where a Mansard Fits in Hounslow

Hounslow's oldest streets are along the river: Old Chiswick, Strand-on-the-Green, Brentford and Old Isleworth, where Georgian and early Victorian houses sit alongside later terraces. Hounslow has many conservation areas here, each with its own appraisal.1 Where a street like these already has mansard roofs, that history is exactly what Hounslow's guidelines look for.

The guidelines say that normally a mansard-style extension will not fit in with the character of the area. However, in areas with older house styles and a historical precedent, planning permission may be given for an attractive mansard that complements the house and street scene.2 Hounslow also says it will normally refuse roof extensions that raise the ridge or that are as wide as the house, so the mansard has to stay within the existing roof height.

Planning in Hounslow

Planning a Mansard in Hounslow

  • Full planning permission is needed for almost every mansard.
  • Historic precedent on your street or nearby is essential under Hounslow's guidelines.2
  • No ridge raising: roof extensions above the existing ridge are normally refused.
  • Bedford Park and Gunnersbury Park: Article 4 directions cover every property, and mansards are very unlikely.3

If a mansard is not realistic, Hounslow's guidelines describe what a conservation-area rear dormer should look like instead: less than half the width of the original roof, set down a metre from the ridge and a metre above the eaves, centrally placed, and sometimes with a pitched or hipped roof and traditional detailing such as ornamental bargeboards.
