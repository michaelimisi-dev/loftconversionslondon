Local knowledge

Mansards on Wandsworth's Victorian Terraces

The densest parts of Wandsworth, around Clapham Junction, Battersea and Wandsworth Town, are lined with two-storey Victorian terraces that have shallow slate roofs. A dormer on these roofs can feel cramped, which is why many owners look at a rear mansard instead. Where several houses on a street already have mansards, a new one that matches their height and slope is generally easier to justify.

Wandsworth also has some of London's most tightly controlled small-house estates. The Shaftesbury Park Estate, built between 1872 and 1877, and the Latchmere Estate, built by Battersea's council in 1903 as one of the first council estates built with directly employed labour, are both conservation areas with Article 4 directions covering roofs.1, 2 Mansards are rarely appropriate on these cottages, and we will tell you so honestly at the survey.

Planning a Mansard in Wandsworth

  • Full planning permission is needed for almost every mansard.

  • Conservation areas: we read the relevant appraisal and design to it, and check whether your area is one of the eight with an Article 4 direction.3
  • Council guidance: Wandsworth's Housing SPD sets out its approach to roof alterations and loft conversions.4
