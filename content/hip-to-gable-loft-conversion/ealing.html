Local knowledge

Ealing's Position on Hip-to-Gables

Between the wars, Ealing's western and northern districts filled with semi-detached houses with hipped roofs, and they are exactly where a hip-to-gable is most useful. Ealing Council's residential extensions guidance, however, says that if you want to turn a hipped roof into a gable on a semi-detached or terraced house, planning permission, if required, will normally be refused, because it unbalances the symmetry of the pair and harms the character of the area.1

The key words are "if required". Outside conservation areas, many hip-to-gables are permitted development and need no application at all.2 Inside them, the picture is much tougher. In the Brunswick Conservation Area, a 1930s estate of semis designated in 2004, the guidance says changing a hipped roof to a gable should be avoided and names hip-to-gables as its biggest concern, noting that converting both halves of a pair at once is far less jarring than one.3
