Local knowledge

Where Hip-to-Gable Conversions Work in Wandsworth

Unlike the outer London suburbs, Wandsworth has relatively few streets made up entirely of hipped-roof semis. Most of its housing is Victorian and Edwardian terraces. Hipped roofs here tend to appear on the end house of a terrace, on Edwardian and interwar semi-detached pairs in Southfields, Putney and the Earlsfield borders, and on larger detached houses near the commons.

That makes each Wandsworth hip-to-gable a little different, because the gable has to sit comfortably against the rest of the terrace or its matching pair. The council has approved this kind of work: for example, a hip-to-gable roof with rear roof extensions on Gressenhall Road in Southfields.1 Where one half of a semi-detached pair converts, we pay close attention to how the new gable reads against the untouched half next door.

Planning in Wandsworth

Planning a Hip-to-Gable in Wandsworth

  • Permitted development: a hip-to-gable counts towards the 40 cubic metre (end-of-terrace) or 50 cubic metre (semi-detached) volume limit, and is exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back.2
  • Conservation areas: roof extensions need planning permission. On estates with Article 4 directions, such as Totterdown Fields in Tooting, changes to roofs are specifically controlled.3
  • Lawful development certificate: recommended even when no application is needed, so a future buyer's solicitor has proof.
