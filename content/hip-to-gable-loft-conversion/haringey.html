Local knowledge

Hipped Roofs in Haringey

Most of southern and eastern Haringey is Victorian terraces with no side roof to convert. The borough's hipped roofs are mainly in the west and north: Edwardian and interwar semi-detached houses around Muswell Hill, Fortis Green and Bounds Green, and larger detached houses towards Highgate. These are where hip-to-gable conversions make the biggest difference, because a hipped roof can leave a loft too narrow for a staircase.

The complication is that much of this area is protected. Muswell Hill Conservation Area was designated in 1974 and extended in 1991, and Haringey has 28 conservation areas in all.1, 2 Inside them, changing a hipped roof to a gable alters exactly the kind of consistent roofline the council looks to protect, so a planning application is needed and a rear dormer may be the better route. Outside them, a hip-to-gable is often permitted development.3 On a detached Highgate or Muswell Hill house with hips on both sides, converting just one side is often enough for the staircase and keeps the roof looking balanced.

Planning in Haringey

Planning a Hip-to-Gable in Haringey

  • Outside conservation areas: often permitted development within the 40 or 50 cubic metre limit, and exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back.3
  • Inside conservation areas: a planning application is needed, and the effect on the street's roofline is central to the decision.
  • Front roof slopes: generally treated as unsuitable for extensions in Haringey, so the gable is kept to the side and the dormer to the rear.
