Local knowledge

Hounslow's Interwar Semis

The western half of Hounslow borough grew quickly between the wars. Hounslow's own conservation area appraisals describe Feltham's housing as mostly late interwar, from 1930 to 1939, and post-war, and Hounslow West as predominantly interwar and early post-war two-storey terraces and semi-detached houses with a classic English suburban character.1, 2 Most of those semis have hipped roofs, where a hip-to-gable gives the loft enough width for a proper staircase.

Hounslow's residential extension guidelines are firm in conservation areas: hip-to-gable roof extensions will not normally be acceptable there, because they alter the building's original proportions and the character of the street.3 Most of Feltham and Hounslow West sits outside conservation areas, though, where a hip-to-gable within 50 cubic metres is usually permitted development.4

Planning in Hounslow

Planning a Hip-to-Gable in Hounslow

  • Permitted development: within 50 cubic metres on a semi or 40 on an end-of-terrace, below the ridge, and exempt from the eaves set-back.4
  • Conservation areas: hip-to-gables not normally acceptable. Small side dormers may be considered.3
  • No wrap-around dormers: where planning is needed, side and rear dormers must stay separate.

In a conservation area, the alternatives have their own rules too. Hounslow will only approve side dormers that are small, respect the profile of the original hipped roof and suit the style of the house. It says dormers on both sides of a hipped roof, side dormers on exposed corner sites and any that remove a chimney are unlikely to be acceptable.
