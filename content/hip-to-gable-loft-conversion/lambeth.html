Local knowledge

Hipped Roofs in Lambeth's Suburbs

Lambeth's Design Guide notes that hipped roofs are common on detached houses, at the ends of some terraces and on semi-detached pairs, and are particularly common in its suburban areas. On Victorian houses the pitches are generally low, while interwar houses often have plain clay-tiled roofs.1 In practice that means Streatham, West Norwood and the southern parts of Brixton and Tulse Hill, rather than the denser terraces further north.

The guide is explicit about these houses. On clay-tiled suburban homes, which it calls characteristic of suburban Lambeth, a full hip-to-gable will be resisted in favour of a half-hip, which keeps more of the traditional roof shape. Where chimneys end up looking stunted on the enlarged roof, they should be extended upwards.1 On heritage assets and in conservation areas, losing the hipped roof is not considered acceptable at all.

Planning in Lambeth

Planning a Hip-to-Gable in Lambeth

  • Conservation areas and heritage assets: Lambeth does not accept the loss of hipped roofs. A rear dormer or rooflights are the alternatives.1
  • Clay-tiled suburban houses: expect to need a half-hip rather than a full gable.
  • Elsewhere: a hip-to-gable may be permitted development within the 40 or 50 cubic metre limit, and it is exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back.2
