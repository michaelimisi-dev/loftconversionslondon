Local knowledge

Hipped Roofs in Waltham Forest

Southern Waltham Forest is mostly Victorian terraces, but the northern half of the borough grew between the wars. Highams Park, Chingford and the Woodford Green borders have streets of 1920s and 1930s semi-detached houses with hipped roofs. The Warner family, who built so much of Walthamstow, also developed the Highams Estate in the 1930s.1 These are the houses where a hip-to-gable makes the biggest difference.

Waltham Forest's draft Residential Alterations and Retrofit guidance takes a firm line. Where planning permission is needed, hip-to-gable extensions are not usually approved unless there is an established pattern of them nearby. Even then they must be of exemplar design and keep the symmetry of the building or terrace.2 On a street where several pairs have already been converted, the case is much stronger.

Planning in Waltham Forest

Planning a Hip-to-Gable in Waltham Forest

  • Permitted development: within 50 cubic metres on a semi or 40 on an end-of-terrace, below the ridge, and exempt from the eaves set-back.3
  • Planning application: only likely to succeed with an established local pattern and a symmetrical, high-quality design.2
  • Highams Park: the council has made an Article 4 direction for the Highams Area of Special Character, so check your street's current status.4

Waltham Forest also expects any planning-route loft to use the highest quality materials available, chosen to match the existing roof.
