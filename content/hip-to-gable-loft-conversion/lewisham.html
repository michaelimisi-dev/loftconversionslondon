Local knowledge

Hipped Roofs Across Lewisham

The northern half of Lewisham is mostly Victorian terraces, but south of the South Circular the housing opens out. Lee, Grove Park, Downham and parts of Sydenham have interwar semi-detached houses and short terraces with hipped ends, and these are where most of the borough's hip-to-gable conversions happen. Lewisham's SPD lists hipped roofs among the common roof types in the borough, alongside dual-pitched, London, chalet and M-shaped roofs.1

What makes Lewisham distinctive is how much weight it gives to the pair. Its SPD illustrations show a hip-to-gable that keeps a semi-detached pair symmetrical as acceptable outside conservation areas, and one on the end of a terrace as acceptable too. A single converted half that leaves the pair lopsided is where problems arise, unless the sense of space between houses is kept.1

Planning in Lewisham

Planning a Hip-to-Gable in Lewisham

  • Conservation areas: Lewisham says hip-to-gable extensions will not be permitted on a pair of semi-detached houses in a conservation area.1
  • Outside conservation areas: often permitted development within 50 cubic metres for a semi or 40 for an end-of-terrace, and exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back.2
  • Article 4 areas: Lewisham's directions can control roof alterations, so we check your street against its list.3
