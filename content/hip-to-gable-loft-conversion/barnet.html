Local knowledge

Barnet's Interwar Semis and Their Hipped Roofs

When the Northern line reached Edgware in 1924, the fields of north-west Barnet were sold off plot by plot to speculative builders, and within a decade Edgware, Burnt Oak, Mill Hill and Hendon were covered in semi-detached houses.1 Almost all have hipped roofs, which leave the loft narrow at the side. Squaring off that side with a gable, and usually adding a rear dormer, turns it into a full-width room with space for a staircase.

Because so many pairs have been converted, a hip-to-gable often fits the established character of a Barnet street. Where planning permission is needed, Barnet's 2016 Residential Design Guidance asks that the gable should not appear out of character, a test planning inspectors have applied at appeal.2 On a street where neighbouring pairs already have gables, that is a far easier case to make. Detached houses in Mill Hill and Totteridge can have hips on both sides, and converting both gives the largest loft of all, though it uses up more of the 50 cubic metre allowance.

Planning in Barnet

Planning a Hip-to-Gable in Barnet

  • Permitted development: within 50 cubic metres on a semi or detached house, exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back. Barnet has issued lawful development certificates for hip-to-gable and rear dormer schemes.3, 4
  • Planning application: judged on whether the gable looks out of character with the street.
  • Hampstead Garden Suburb and other Article 4 areas: roof alterations need planning permission.
