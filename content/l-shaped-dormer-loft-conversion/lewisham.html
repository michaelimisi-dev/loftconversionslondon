Local knowledge

L-Shaped Dormers on Lewisham Terraces

The Corbett Estate's roughly 3,000 houses, built from 1895 onwards between Hither Green and Catford, share a repeated plan: a main house with a narrower rear return.1 That rear return is what an L-shaped dormer extends over, so on many Corbett streets the L-shaped loft has become the standard way to add two bedrooms. The same layout continues through much of Ladywell, Catford and Lee.

Lewisham's SPD has a section dedicated to L-shaped roof extensions. It shows a sympathetic, tile-hung example as acceptable and an overbearing one that dominates both the original house and the rear return as unacceptable. It also says L-shaped extensions will be resisted in conservation areas.2

Ladywell and Brockley look similar on the map, but the planning position is different. Both are conservation areas with Article 4 directions on Lewisham's list, so an L-shaped dormer there needs a planning application and faces the council's presumption against them. A few streets away on the Corbett Estate, the same house type may need no application at all, which is why we always start by checking the exact address.

Planning in Lewisham

Planning an L-Shaped Dormer in Lewisham

  • Outside conservation areas: often permitted development within 40 cubic metres on a terrace. The section joining the rear return roof is exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back.3
  • Conservation areas: Lewisham resists L-shaped extensions. A modest rear dormer is the usual alternative.
  • Article 4 areas: roof alterations need planning permission in areas on Lewisham's list.4
