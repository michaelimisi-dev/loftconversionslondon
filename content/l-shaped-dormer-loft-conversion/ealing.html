Local knowledge

L-Shaped Dormers on Ealing's Terraces

When the railway brought commuters to Acton, Ealing and Hanwell in the late 19th century, builders lined the new streets with terraces that had a narrower two-storey back addition for the kitchen and a bedroom above. That back addition, with its own lower roof, is what an L-shaped dormer extends over. Joining a dormer over it to one on the main roof usually gives room for two bedrooms, or a bedroom and a bathroom.

Ealing's residential extensions guidance asks that dormers do not appear unduly obtrusive or dominant and do not take up the entire area of a roof slope.1 Under permitted development, an L-shaped dormer can be larger than that guidance suggests, as long as it stays within 40 cubic metres on a terrace and meets the other conditions.2 Where a planning application is needed, we keep both dormers in proportion with the house so they meet Ealing's expectations.

Planning in Ealing

Planning an L-Shaped Dormer in Ealing

  • Permitted development: often possible within 40 cubic metres on a terrace. The section joining the back addition roof is exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back.2
  • Lawful development certificate: recommended by Ealing to confirm permitted development work is lawful.1
  • Conservation areas: a planning application is needed, and permitted development rights may not apply.
