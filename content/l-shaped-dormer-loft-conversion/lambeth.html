Local knowledge

L-Shaped Dormers on Lambeth Terraces

Behind the front walls of Brixton, Herne Hill and the Clapham borders are thousands of late Victorian and Edwardian terraces with a narrower two-storey rear return. Lambeth calls an L-shaped dormer a rear roof extension that projects out over that rear return, and says the aim should be to integrate it with the main roof and keep it subordinate to the house.1

The council's guidance is detailed. The L-shaped part should hold only one storey and stay below the main ridge. The end gable of the rear return should be kept, with slate hanging that follows its line. Party walls should rise in stock brick with a traditional parapet and brick-on-edge coping, and chimneys on the party walls should be extended at least eight brick courses above the roof.1 If you are also considering a rear mansard, Lambeth asks for both to be designed together.

Planning in Lambeth

Planning an L-Shaped Dormer in Lambeth

  • Conservation areas and heritage assets: Lambeth does not consider L-shaped rear return extensions appropriate. A modest rear dormer is the usual alternative.1
  • Elsewhere: an L-shaped dormer may be permitted development within 40 cubic metres on a terrace, and the section joining the rear return is exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back.2
  • Neighbours: Lambeth checks for overlooking and loss of daylight from new windows, so we position them carefully.
