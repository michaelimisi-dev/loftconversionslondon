Local knowledge

Why Wandsworth Terraces Suit L-Shaped Dormers

Much of Tooting, Balham and Earlsfield was built in a rush between the 1880s and 1910s, as railway and tram links reached south-west London. Builders used the same plan again and again: a two-storey main house with a narrower back addition holding the kitchen and a bedroom or bathroom above. That outrigger roof is what turns a single rear dormer into an L, and on many Wandsworth streets the L-shaped dormer has become the standard loft conversion.

Tooting is also home to Totterdown Fields, an early 1900s London County Council cottage estate now protected as a conservation area with an Article 4 direction covering changes to roofs.1 Its cottages follow a different, more uniform pattern from the surrounding terraces, so L-shaped dormers there need a planning application and a careful, sympathetic design.

Planning in Wandsworth

Planning an L-Shaped Dormer in Wandsworth

  • Permitted development: possible within 40 cubic metres on a terrace. The section joining the outrigger roof is exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back.2
  • Conservation areas and Article 4 estates: a planning application is required.3
  • Neighbours: overlooking from the outrigger dormer's windows into gardens next door is the most common concern, so we place windows thoughtfully.

On many Tooting and Earlsfield terraces the outrigger roof is lower and narrower than the main roof, so the floor of the L section often sits a step below the main loft. We plan that change in level at the survey, either as a single step on the landing or by adjusting the joists, so the two rooms feel like one floor.
