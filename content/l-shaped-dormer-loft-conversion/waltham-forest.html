Local knowledge

L-Shaped Dormers on Waltham Forest Terraces

Once the railway reached Walthamstow around 1870, the fields of Walthamstow and Leyton were covered over the following forty years with two-storey terraces for City workers, many of them built by the Warner family's companies.1 Most of these houses have a narrower back addition with its own lower roof, and joining a dormer over it to one on the main roof is how so many Waltham Forest families have added two bedrooms.

Waltham Forest's draft Residential Alterations and Retrofit guidance addresses them directly: rear L-shaped dormers may be supported where they reflect the local character and are of exemplar design, using the highest quality materials available.2 On streets where L-shaped dormers are already common, the local character supports another one; on an untouched terrace, the design has to work harder.

Planning in Waltham Forest

Planning an L-Shaped Dormer in Waltham Forest

  • Permitted development: within 40 cubic metres on a terrace. The section joining the back addition roof is exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back.3
  • Planning application: supported where it reflects local character and is of exemplar design.2
  • Flats: upper flats, including many Warner flats, need planning permission because permitted development does not apply.
  • Conservation areas: such as Walthamstow Village, where Article 4 directions apply.4
