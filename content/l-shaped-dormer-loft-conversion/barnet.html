Local knowledge

L-Shaped Dormers on Barnet's Older Streets

Before the Northern line extension of the 1920s, Barnet's built-up areas clustered around older railway stations: the Great Northern line through Finchley, East Finchley and New Barnet, and the Midland line at Cricklewood and Hendon. The Victorian and Edwardian terraces built around them were designed with a narrower rear return, and that rear return roof is what an L-shaped dormer extends over.

On these streets an L-shaped dormer is often the best way to get two rooms from the loft. Built under permitted development, it can be generous, provided it stays within 40 cubic metres on a terrace.1 If planning permission is needed, Barnet's 2016 Residential Design Guidance expects dormers to be subordinate features on the roof, which planning inspectors have applied when assessing roof extensions at appeal.2 That can mean noticeably smaller dormers, which is why the route matters. On streets of near-identical terraces, such as those around Cricklewood, an L-shaped dormer that matches ones already built by neighbours is usually the easiest kind to get right.

Planning in Barnet

Planning an L-Shaped Dormer in Barnet

  • Permitted development: within 40 cubic metres on a terrace or 50 on a semi. The section joining the rear return roof is exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back.1
  • Planning application: designed to Barnet's 2016 SPD with subordinate dormers.
  • Article 4 areas: Barnet has 10 conservation areas covered by Article 4 directions.3
