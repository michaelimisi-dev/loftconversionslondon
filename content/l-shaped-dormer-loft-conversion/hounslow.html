Local knowledge

L-Shaped Dormers and Hounslow's Rules

Hounslow's Victorian terraces, from the Glebe Estate in Chiswick to the streets around Brentford and Isleworth, were often built in an L shape with a two-storey rear projection, or outrigger.1, 2 That outrigger roof is what an L-shaped dormer extends over, and in most London boroughs it is the standard way to get two rooms from the loft.

Hounslow is different. Its residential extension guidelines say the council will normally refuse roof extensions over two-storey outriggers or rear additions, and in conservation areas roof extensions over rear outriggers will normally be refused.2 Those guidelines govern planning applications, not permitted development. So on a house that still has its permitted development rights, an L-shaped dormer within 40 cubic metres can usually go ahead without an application at all.3

Planning in Hounslow

Planning an L-Shaped Dormer in Hounslow

  • Permitted development: the realistic route, within 40 cubic metres on a terrace, with the outrigger section exempt from the eaves set-back.3
  • Planning application: outrigger roof extensions normally refused, so a main-roof dormer only is the usual alternative.2
  • Conservation areas and Article 4 areas: including Bedford Park and Gunnersbury Park, where permitted development rights are removed.4

If a planning application is unavoidable, Hounslow's guidelines point to a single rear dormer on the main roof, set centrally on the roof face and kept clear of the ridge, eaves and sides.
