Local knowledge

L-Shaped Dormers on Haringey Terraces

Haringey's Victorian terraces were built fast in the last two decades of the 19th century. The Harringay Ladder alone was laid out in 1880 to 1881 and fully built by 1900, with several builders working side by side under the British Land Company's covenants.1 Almost all of these houses have a back addition, and the L-shaped dormer, joining a dormer on the main roof with one over the back addition, has become the standard way to convert them.

That variety matters. One builder's back addition may be a storey lower or a metre narrower than the next, which changes the floor levels, headroom and where the stair can land. We survey your house rather than assuming the street pattern. In conservation areas such as Crouch End or Noel Park, an L-shaped dormer needs planning permission, and the council looks at how it affects the consistent roofline.2 Where the back addition is wide enough, the section over it can hold a full bathroom rather than just a shower room; we measure it at the survey so you know which is possible before choosing a layout.

Planning in Haringey

Planning an L-Shaped Dormer in Haringey

  • Outside conservation areas: often permitted development within 40 cubic metres on a terrace. The part joining the back addition roof is exempt from the 20 cm eaves set-back.3
  • Conservation areas: a planning application is required.
  • Converted houses: if your house has been split into flats, permitted development does not apply.
